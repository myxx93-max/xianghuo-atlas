# Multilingual Sacred-Terms Glossary

The glossary is a bridge between languages and traditions—not a universal dictionary of gods.

Its purpose is to preserve original terms, scripts, pronunciation, context, translation choices, and limits of equivalence. It helps contributors discover how a term is used in a particular community or source, without allowing a convenient English label to replace the source tradition's own categories.

## Non-negotiable rules

1. **One term, one contextual record.** A word used in different traditions, periods, or communities may require separate records.
2. **Original form first.** Preserve original script, diacritics, transliteration, and known variants.
3. **No universal one-word translations.** “God”, “deity”, “spirit”, “sacred”, and “divine” are often imperfect approximations.
4. **No equivalence by vocabulary alone.** Similar translations do not establish shared identity, origin, function, or theology.
5. **Attribute explanations.** Distinguish community self-description, practitioner testimony, primary texts, and scholarly interpretation.
6. **Keep uncertainty visible.** Use UNKNOWN, CONTESTED, or NEEDS_COMMUNITY_REVIEW rather than filling gaps by inference.
7. **Respect restrictions.** Some names, pronunciations, texts, or knowledge may not be appropriate for public display.

## Starter coverage

The first records are deliberately small and uneven. They are seed entries, not a complete lexicon and not community-approved definitions. Source links support only the limited notes attached to each record; they do not settle all theological or linguistic interpretations.

## How to contribute

Open an issue or pull request with the original term and script; language and specific tradition/community; pronunciation or transliteration; source-specific contextual explanation; preferred translation and why; translations considered misleading; source, date, edition, and page/section; what should not be inferred or published; and consent and rights information.

A contributor may write in their own language. Translation into English can be added later without replacing the original contribution.
