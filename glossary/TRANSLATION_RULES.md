# Translation and Cross-Tradition Equivalence Rules

## 1. Keep four layers separate

For every glossary entry, distinguish:

1. **Lexical gloss:** a short dictionary-like rendering.
2. **Contextual meaning:** what the term does in a particular source, community, ritual, or period.
3. **Practitioner explanation:** how a contributor or community member explains it.
4. **Scholarly interpretation:** how a named researcher analyzes it.

These layers may agree, differ, or conflict. Do not silently merge them.

## 2. Translation is not identity

If two terms are both translated as “god”, that does not prove they refer to equivalent beings or concepts. A translator's choice is evidence about a translation, not proof of metaphysical or historical identity.

## 3. Use explicit relation labels

Recommended labels:
- GLOSSED_AS_IN_TRANSLATION
- TRANSLITERATION_OF
- VARIANT_FORM_OF
- USED_IN_TEXT
- EXPLAINED_BY_COMMUNITY_AS
- CONTRASTED_WITH
- COMPARED_BY_SCHOLAR_TO
- CLAIMED_EQUIVALENT_BY_SOURCE
- DISPUTED_EQUIVALENCE_WITH
- UNKNOWN_RELATION

Every cross-tradition comparison must name its source or contributor and state the comparison dimension.

## 4. Preserve grammar and number

Record singular/plural behavior, grammatical gender where relevant, honorifics, case endings, titles, and whether the term normally appears with a proper name. Do not assume English grammatical categories apply.

## 5. Respect untranslatable or restricted terms

A community may prefer that a term remain untranslated, unpronounced in certain contexts, or absent from public indexes. Record that boundary without exposing the protected content.

## 6. No ranking

Do not score terms by spiritual power, truth, purity, age, sophistication, or importance. Popularity and search volume are not measures of religious validity.

## 7. Correction is a first-class contribution

A community correction can change a preferred translation, add a restriction, or split one overly broad entry into several context-specific records. Preserve version history and attribution.
