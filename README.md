# Xianghuo Atlas (香火图谱)

**A plural, evidence-aware atlas of sacred beings, traditions, places, practices, stories, and human aspirations.**

Xianghuo Atlas is an independent cultural-research project. It invites people from different religious, spiritual, cultural, and non-religious backgrounds to document how their own communities name, understand, remember, represent, and relate to sacred beings and realities.

It is **not** an attempt to combine all religions into one pantheon, rank traditions, identify supposedly equivalent gods, prove or disprove religious claims, or convert living traditions into fictional worldbuilding.

## Why this project exists

Cultural records are fragmented across local names, oral accounts, inscriptions, family memory, ritual practice, migration, art, and texts. A name may survive while its context is lost. Similar figures may be treated as identical without evidence. A tradition may be described using categories that its practitioners would not use.

The atlas aims to preserve these distinctions while making carefully sourced connections possible.

## A plural approach

The Chinese working name *Xianghuo Atlas* does not assume that every culture has an equivalent concept of *xianghuo* (incense, offerings, continuity of worship, or the social life around a shrine). The term is a point of departure, not a universal translation.

Likewise, **deity**, **神格**, **kami**, **deva**, **saint**, **ancestor**, **spirit**, **神明**, and **sacred presence** are not interchangeable labels. Preserve community terms and explain translations rather than silently normalizing them.

Some traditions are polytheistic; some are monotheistic; some are non-theistic or do not organize sacred reality around gods; many contain internal diversity and historical change. The atlas supports different record types instead of forcing every tradition into a deity/pantheon table.

## Core principles

1. **Tradition-first terminology:** retain self-designations and local names; add comparative translations only as secondary descriptions.
2. **No forced equivalence:** resemblance is not identity, shared origin, or historical transmission.
3. **Evidence-aware relations:** distinguish direct observation, practitioner testimony, textual attestation, scholarly interpretation, and open hypothesis.
4. **Plurality within traditions:** do not treat a religion, people, or region as internally uniform.
5. **Living-community respect:** follow community protocols for restricted, sacred, private, or sensitive knowledge.
6. **Transparent uncertainty:** preserve disagreement, gaps, conflicting versions, and UNKNOWN states.
7. **No ranking or proselytizing:** the atlas does not evaluate which faith is true, superior, older, or more powerful.
8. **Consent and rights:** publish only material with appropriate permission, clear provenance, and lawful reuse conditions.

## What can be documented?

- names, titles, epithets, and language variants;
- community-specific understandings of sacred beings or realities;
- places, shrines, landscapes, objects, and material culture;
- stories, hymns, prayers, inscriptions, and textual lineages;
- ritual practices, festivals, offerings, and forms of remembrance;
- migration, adaptation, historical contact, and changes in practice;
- human aspirations and social contexts, where contributors consider them appropriate;
- contested interpretations, absences, and evidence gaps.

## What this project does not do

- claim that all gods are versions of one another;
- map a monotheistic God into a polytheistic pantheon;
- reduce Buddhist traditions to a list of gods;
- treat Indigenous sacred knowledge as open data;
- publish restricted ritual details, private family knowledge, or sensitive locations without permission;
- offer divination, spiritual services, ritual transactions, or claims of supernatural efficacy.

## Current status

This is an open conceptual and documentation framework. It is not yet a verified global database, a complete survey of world religions, or a claim of academic consensus. Initial tradition notes are orientation aids and require review by knowledgeable practitioners and scholars.

## Invitation

If you know how your community names and understands sacred beings, ancestors, spirits, places, stories, or ultimate realities, we welcome corrections and contributions in your own terms. You do not need to agree with the project's terminology. Explaining why a term does not fit your tradition is a valuable contribution.

**Help us build an atlas that preserves difference rather than erasing it.**
