# Community Contribution, Consent, and Care

Xianghuo Atlas is intended to be plural and collaborative. Contributors are not expected to agree on theology or metaphysics. The shared task is careful description, traceable sourcing, and respect for community boundaries.

## Who can contribute?

Practitioners and community members; historians, anthropologists, religious-studies scholars, linguists, and archivists; local historians and cultural workers; translators and language specialists; and people who can identify errors or explain why a category does not fit.

You may contribute in your own language. Preserve original terms and scripts where possible.

## What is welcome?

- corrections to names, pronunciation, translation, and context;
- public sources and bibliographic references;
- documented local variants and historical changes;
- explanations of how your community understands a figure, place, text, or practice;
- counterexamples to proposed comparisons;
- notes about what should not be recorded or shared.

## Consent and restrictions

Before contributing personal testimony, photographs, recordings, family documents, or community knowledge, confirm that you have the right and permission to share them.

Do not submit private or identifying information without consent; restricted, initiatory, sacred, or confidential knowledge; precise locations of vulnerable sacred sites where disclosure could cause harm; images or recordings without reuse permission; or material obtained by trespass, deception, or violation of community rules.

A public web page is not automatically free to reuse. Provide citation and license information.

## Attribution and licensing

Contributors may request attribution under a chosen name, organization, or pseudonym. Do not submit material unless you can grant the project the permissions required for the proposed publication. Preserve contributor credit and stated license. Where permission is limited, keep material private or record only a non-sensitive bibliographic pointer.

## Disagreement and correction

Disagreement is not a defect to be erased. Preserve distinct community accounts rather than forcing consensus. Record who makes a claim and what source supports it. Mark uncertain relationships as hypotheses. Allow corrections, withdrawal, and restriction requests. Do not treat majority votes, popularity, or star ratings as evidence of religious truth.

## Moderation

The project will not host harassment, sectarian attacks, proselytizing campaigns, claims of spiritual superiority, threats, or attempts to expose restricted knowledge. Criticism of documentation methods and scholarly interpretations is welcome when expressed respectfully.
